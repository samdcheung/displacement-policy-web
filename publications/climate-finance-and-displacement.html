Abstract

Climate-related displacement presents a public-finance challenge extending beyond individual projects, disaster-risk payouts and international funding commitments. Population movement changes where public expenditure is needed, how responsibilities are distributed across institutions and jurisdictions, and how long public services must be sustained.

Published following COP31, this policy brief examines how existing climate-finance instruments can better support the fiscal and institutional responsibilities arising from displacement. It identifies temporal, territorial and economic dimensions of displacement-related fiscal risk and applies a functional framework encompassing fiscal space, institutional adaptation and sustainable public responsibilities.

Drawing on emerging climate-risk and fiscal methodologies, the brief proposes stronger integration of displacement into adaptation planning, public-finance management and development-finance operations, including more coordinated preparation of credible country-owned financing responses.
